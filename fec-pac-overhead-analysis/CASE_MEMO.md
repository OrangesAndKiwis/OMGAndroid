## Q1 — Did the Animal Protection PAC flag? Yes, but it's a weak anchor.

**ANIMAL PROTECTION PAC (FEC C00878165)** *is* flagged: 6.6% of spend to
candidates, 70% overhead, ~32% cost-to-raise in 2024. But on our composite lead
score it lands near the **bottom (1–2 of 11)**, for reasons that matter to the case:

- Its money went to **ordinary Democratic list/digital fundraising firms**
  (Break Something, Blue Action) — **not** a fundraiser tied to any fraud finding.
- No self-dealing, no convicted-operator vendor, "only" ~32% cost-to-raise.

So it is the **archetype that triggered your instinct** (emotive $1 appeals,
most money kept) but a **mild instance** — legally the hardest kind to win on,
because high overhead alone is protected (see §Q4). Use it as the *illustration*
of a widespread pattern, not the lead plaintiff's committee. The strong cases are
the ones below, where the same pattern comes with **provable affirmative
deception and, in several, criminal convictions.**

## Q2 — The PACs that actually fit, tiered by case strength

The winnable theory is **affirmative misrepresentation** (donors told the money
helps police/veterans/sick kids/candidates when ~90% doesn't), *not* "overhead
too high." Rank targets by how provable that deception is:

### Tier 1 — Fraud already adjudicated (strongest)
The fundraising **vendor** is the common enterprise, and it's been criminally
convicted — so the deception is largely proven and may support collateral estoppel.

| PAC (in our 2024 data) | 2024 receipts | To candidates | Common operator |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| Police Coalition of America | $3.3M | 1.3% | **Cloud Data / Zeitlin** |
| Police & Trooper Support PAC | $1.8M | 1.5% | **Cloud Data / Zeitlin** |
| American Veterans Initiative PAC | $1.6M | 2.5% | **Cloud Data / Zeitlin** |
| Law Enforcement for a Safer America | $8.4M* | ~0% | **Cloud Data / Zeitlin** |

*Richard **Zeitlin pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit wire fraud** (Sept
2024), sentenced to **121 months** — for **altering call scripts so donors
thought they were giving to charities/causes**, while his firms kept ~90%. That
is the exact deception a civil class would plead, already established.*

### Tier 2 — Vendor operator convicted (strong)
| God, Family, & Country PAC | $0.4M | ~0% | **Better Mousetrap / Jack Daly (convicted mail fraud)** |
| Elect Republicans | $0.3M | ~0% | **Better Mousetrap / Jack Daly** |

*Daly's prosecution specifically cited **targeting "a woman with Alzheimer's and
elderly veterans."** Both PACs route donor money to his firm.*

### Tier 3 — Documented "faux charity" / insider pattern (needs its own deception proof)
- **American Coalition for Crisis Relief** ($4.9M, ~0% to mission) — Gelvan-linked
  "faux charity" for cancer/veterans.
- **BAMPAC** ($2.2M, ~1%) — treasurer paid himself ~$2M (Center for Public
  Integrity documented).

### Illustrative
- **Animal Protection PAC** — the trigger; use to humanize the pattern, not anchor it.

**Strategic takeaways for framing:**
1. **Sue the vendor/operator, not (only) the PAC.** The for-profit fundraising
   firm is where the ~90% lands, lacks the PAC's speech/nonprofit shields, and is
   the common defendant across many PACs = a natural class defendant.
2. **Lead with the criminal-conviction networks (Zeitlin, Daly).** Fraud is
   already found; the civil case rides an established record.
3. **Plead a uniform misrepresentation** (the scripted "your money helps
   police/veterans/the cause") to support classwide materiality.

## Q3 — Is there real money here? Yes — hundreds of millions, and growing.

Not a phantom. Concrete, sourced:

- **Cumulative ~$344M** across 61 scam PACs (2001–2018), **~72% siphoned to
  overhead** (Issue One / Center for Fiscal Policy).
- **Growth:** 7 PACs (~$5M) in 2001–02 → 61 (~$101M) in 2017–18 → **86 in 2022**
  (OpenSecrets). DOJ/FBI/state-AG warnings and a proposed federal **SCAM PAC Act
  (H.R. 6893)** confirm it's a recognized, expanding problem.
- **Individual networks are huge:** **Zeitlin ~$140–153M** (kept ~87–90%);
  **Gelvan ~$118M**; **Mackenzie $46M** (50+ PACs); **Conservative Majority Fund
  $10M raised / $48k to candidates.**
- **Recovery has been pennies on the dollar:** Zeitlin restitution **$8.9M** vs.
  $130M+ kept; Daly **~$70k**; Gelvan's **$56M** civil judgment largely suspended
  as uncollectable. *That gap between money taken and money recovered is the
  civil-litigation opportunity.*
- **Victims are systematically elderly/fixed-income** (AARP treats scam PACs as
  elder fraud) — sympathetic class, aligned with your consumer-protection framing.

From *our own screen*, in one cycle and just the 752-PAC analyzed band:
**41 PACs fit the "≥40% cost-to-raise + <10% to candidates" profile (~$52M in
2024 receipts); 10 pay a convicted-operator vendor (~$22.5M).** The full flagged
universe is far larger and recurs every cycle.

## Q4 — Is it actionable? The honest landscape (counsel to verify)

**The doorway is open but narrow, and no *private* class action has yet won.**

**Favorable:**
- ***Illinois ex rel. Madigan v. Telemarketing Associates*, 538 U.S. 600 (2003)**
  — the First Amendment does **not** shield **affirmative, knowing
  misrepresentations** to donors. This is the theory of the case.
- ***WinRed v. Ellison*, 59 F.4th 934 (8th Cir. 2023)** — **rejected FECA
  preemption** of a state consumer-protection action against a federal
  PAC-fundraising operation. Generally-applicable fraud/UDAP law is not preempted.
- **Criminal template** (Zeitlin/Daly) shows DOJ treats "lied about where the
  money goes" as ordinary wire/mail fraud — the same conduct a civil suit targets.

**Adverse / hurdles:**
- ***Schaumburg* (444 U.S. 620) and *Riley* (487 U.S. 781)** — you **cannot** win
  on the **overhead percentage itself**; a PAC has **no duty** to spend a minimum
  on candidates. The case must live on **provable deception**, not the ratio.
- **The one on-point private class action — a TCPA robocall suit against the
  Zeitlin network (M.D. Pa. No. 1:21-cv-01668) — was DISMISSED** for failure to
  tie the defendants to the specific calls, *even while they faced criminal
  charges.* Cautionary.
- **Class certification chokepoint:** common-law fraud/many UDAP claims require
  **reliance and materiality**, often individualized (donors heard different
  scripts) → threatens Rule 23(b)(3) predominance. **Statute selection is
  decisive** — favor state UDAP statutes allowing **classwide/presumed reliance**.
- **FTC can't reach a genuine PAC** (15 U.S.C. § 44 nonprofit limit) — only the
  for-profit vendor. So no federal UDAP hammer against the PAC itself.
- Every accountability win so far is a **government actor** (DOJ criminal; state
  AG/FTC civil) under a scienter or clear-and-convincing standard — not a private
  consumer class.

**Most promising posture (for counsel to assess):** a **state-UDAP + common-law
fraud + unjust-enrichment** class, **against the fundraising vendor and
operators** of a **criminal-conviction network** (Zeitlin/Daly PACs), filed in a
**donor-friendly UDAP jurisdiction**, pleading a **uniform scripted
misrepresentation**, riding the established criminal record. Also worth a look:
partnering with / referring to **state AGs** (the proven civil winners) and the
**TCPA** angle (statutory damages, but plead call-traceability carefully).
